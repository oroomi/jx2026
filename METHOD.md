# Dated-event research method (the stitcher)

How `radar/adapters/stitcher.py` finds dated public events for one company. The rule throughout: an LLM proposes, plain code decides, and every decision is logged in `stitch_log` (`radar/schema/040_stitch_log.sql`).

## 1. Queries (search step)
They are built only from register facts already in the store. A personal name is never used.
- `"<legal name>"`, `"<business ID>"`, `"<FI VAT number>"`, `"<domain>"`, `site:<domain>`, `site:<domain> news OR press OR customers OR partners`
- `"<core name>" funding OR investment OR grant`, `"<core name>" pilot OR customer OR contract OR award`
- `"<core name>" EU project OR Horizon OR BlueInvest OR "Business Finland"`
- Finnish companies add `"<core name>" rahoitus OR asiakas OR pilotti OR palkinto OR kiihdyttämö OR sijoitus OR hanke`.
- **Other names of the company** (added 17 Oct for recall): every name the trade register holds for the business ID (PRH/YTJ open API: main, parallel, auxiliary and earlier names), and the name the declared cohort list uses. Each gets `"<name>"`, `"<name>" funding OR investment OR pilot OR customer OR award OR accelerator` and, for Finnish companies, the Finnish line. The prompt lists them as "Also known as". A name shaped like a private person's name is never used.
- When checking a page, a registered name names the company as strongly as the legal name (`registered_name`, high confidence with a day or month). A declared cohort name is `declared_name` (medium). A declared name that the list itself marks as an unconfirmed link (WAIing for Taikoa Oy) widens the search but never names the company on a page.
- The three companies with 0 proposals on 16 Oct trade under other names: Arctic Fry Oy is WESI Solutions (auxiliary name), Sub Marine Store Oy also holds Suomen Varavirta (auxiliary name), Taikoa Oy is behind WAIing (declared, unconfirmed). Each went from 8 to 13 queries.

The core name is the folded name without the legal form (`openalex.core_name`). Both our queries and the queries the LLM reports it ran are logged.

## 2. Sources trusted (read step)
`claude -p --model sonnet` with WebSearch and WebFetch gets the prompt from `build_prompt()`. Print it with `--print-prompt`. The rules it must follow:
- Every event carries the exact URL it opened, and that page must name the company and print the date.
- Preferred sources are the company's own site, press releases, EU sources (CORDIS, BlueInvest), Business Finland, patent offices and news.
- Refused, and also dropped by code: LinkedIn, Facebook, Instagram, X, Crunchbase, Dealroom, PitchBook, Tracxn, CB Insights, Nordic9.
- The quote is at most 20 verbatim words and names the company. A sentence that names a private person is never used. Code also redacts person-shaped names to `[name]`.
- The signal type must be one of the emittable types: the existing types in `radar/emit.py` that a web page can show, plus `programme_entry`, `award` and `funding_round` (the chief's decision on 17 Oct: the thesis feed already counts them; registered in the stitcher the way `ecosystem.py` registers its types). Anything else is `unmapped` plus a suggested label: it stays in `stitch_log` as `not_emitted` and is never a signal. A saved answer that says `unmapped` with one of the three as its label is read as that type.

The answer is cached under `data/cache/stitcher/llm/` by prompt hash. A saved answer can be replayed with `--proposals DIR`.

## 3. Dating (verify step, no LLM)
- The code fetches each URL itself: 1 request per 2 s, cached under `data/cache/stitcher/pages/`, PDFs read with pdftotext.
- **Kept only if** the page names the company and prints the date or its year. Naming means, strongest first: business ID or FI VAT number, then the legal name, then the core name as whole words, then the website domain.
- Dates are matched in ISO, Finnish `d.m.yyyy`, `d/m/yyyy`, and month names in English, Finnish, Swedish, Danish, German and Spanish, including Finnish inflections.
- The stored precision is what the page supports and never more than proposed. A day the page does not print becomes month or year, and the log says so.
- Stored dates: a month-only date is the last day of the month, as `ecosystem.py` does. A year-only date is 1 January with a `signal_meta` row of `year`.
- Confidence follows the rule in `emit.py`. It is high when the business ID or legal name and a day or month are on the page. Otherwise it is medium.
- **public_since** is the Internet Archive's first 200 capture of that exact URL, from CDX. It is the earliest day anyone could have read the event, and it is the honest number for earliness. When CDX cannot be reached, the Wayback availability API is asked for the capture closest to 1 January 1996, which is the earliest. It is used only when that capture is a 200.
- Drop reasons are `source_not_allowed`, `date_in_future`, `fetch_failed: …`, `company_not_on_page`, `date_not_on_page` and `name_and_date_not_on_page`.

## 3b. Adversarial second pass (recheck step, no LLM)
Every kept event is checked again before anything is emitted. The code fetches the page fresh (no cache) and confirms all of these; the first failure drops the event, logged as `verify` / `refuted` with its reason:
- `quote_not_on_page`: the quote is on the re-fetched page word for word (case, accents and punctuation folded). A redacted quote must have each unredacted fragment there, in order.
- `company_not_near_quote`: the company is named in the quote, or within 200 characters of it (business ID, legal name, core name or domain). On the company's own site, the site naming the company is enough.
- `type_not_in_quote`: the quote states an event of the proposed type: it holds one of that type's words (`TYPE_WORDS` in the stitcher, English and Finnish, for example award, prize, won, palkinto for `award`; raised, round, seed, series, invest for `funding_round`; a grant is not a round).
- `nomination_not_award`: a finalist, nomination or shortlist place without a win word is not an award.
- `year_not_near_quote`: a year-only date must be printed within 300 characters of the quote, so a copyright line never dates an event.
- `no_quote`, `refetch_failed: …`.
Passed events are logged `verify` / `confirmed` with what matched. An `unmapped` event is checked for quote and company only and is never emitted. A past run can be rechecked alone with `--recheck-run RUN_ID`. That logs under the old run and emits or removes nothing.

## 4. Emit
Only confirmed events are emitted. Writes go only through `radar.emit.emit_signal` with adapter `stitcher`. The verbatim quote goes into `signal_text` only when it appears word for word on the page and nothing was redacted. `build.py` does not run the stitcher because the module has no `run()`.

## 5. Earliness: counted apart
Stitcher signals never count in the backtest's four groups (live public records, declared research, later, none). `/backtest` lists them under the company as "found by the AI stitcher: never in the live count", and prints one separate line. That line counts cohort companies whose latest stitcher run has an event of an emittable type (an unmapped label such as an incorporation line never counts) that passed both code checks and whose page the Internet Archive captured strictly before the company's reference date. The archive date (`public_since`) counts, never the event date. `radar.adapters.stitcher.archived_before`, called from `radar/earliness.py`, returned as `stitcher` in the backtest result.

## 6. Intake thesis-evidence pass (`--intake-fit`, files only, emits nothing)
For the wide-intake list `tasks/cloud/intake_top100.tsv` (company_key TAB name; these companies are not in the store), the stitcher asks one question per company: is there public evidence that the company itself works in the thesis themes? Register facts come from PRH (website, line of business, other registered names). The LLM answers yes, no or unclear with a quote and a link, at most two searches, and never searches people. Code decides the label:
- **yes** only when the freshly fetched page names the company, holds the quote word for word, and the quote holds a thesis word that is not part of the company's own names. Thesis words come from configuration only: the thesis name keywords, its Hilma keywords and the impact-category words in `config/frameworks.yaml`.
- **no** only when the page names the company and holds the quote.
- Everything else is **unclear**, with the reason.
The checked record per company is `data/fetched/stitcher/intake_fit/<company_key>.json` (company_key, name, thesis_evidence, quote, source_url, public_since, archive_url, thesis_word, checked, llm_answer); the LLM's own answer is kept inside each record (`llm_answer`, with the quote and link when they passed the check), so the repo holds one copy per company. Re-check on the VPS with `python3 -m radar.adapters.stitcher --intake-fit tasks/cloud/intake_top100.tsv` (it reads the answer back from the record; `--proposals DIR` points it at raw answer files instead).

## 7. Ocean Impact Readiness: evidence readiness, not true impact (D27)

The Ocean Impact Assessment on each company page (`radar/ocean_impact.py`, ladder and area table in `impact_readiness` of the thesis files) measures how well a company's ocean impact area is evidenced in public records. It does not measure how much impact the company has. The Ocean Impact Navigator publishes impact areas and KPIs but no levels, and the repo holds no sponsor definition of levels, so the nine rungs are ours and each names the record that must be on file: 1 to 2 the trade register's industry-code family, registered line of business or a corroborated name word point to an area (sector implied only); 3 to 4 the source's own text (a notice title, a project text, an investor-pipeline profile) holds an area word; 5 to 6 a second independent source family names the area and a blue programme or portfolio page lists the company; 7 to 8 declared research holds an impact assessment or a quantified outcome with a stated method, counted only at the dataset's own confidence medium or high; 9 a measured outcome from an official or register source, which no connected source holds yet. The highest rung that passes wins, a company with no record naming an area has no level, and confidence is high when two or more independent source families agree, medium for one family that is more than the sector implied, low otherwise.

**Bands, and the gap.** Levels 1 and 2 carry no band and read "not yet evidenced": the sector is only implied, which is a gap to investigate and not a low score (A'Pelago's guidance of 17 Oct, quoted in README.md). Levels 3 to 7 are Medium and 8 to 9 High; High needs a measured outcome on record, so an assessment alone stays Medium. Scalability is stated only from a register in more than one country, an EU-level programme record or the source's own text about markets, and otherwise reads "not assessed from public records". Every level prints "To raise it", the one record that would lift the rung; at level 5 it names the next rung's record when two source families are already on file, and a second family only when one is (D52).

**One impact truth (D46).** The decision table's impact cell is taken from this ladder: a band of Medium or High is the impact band, and a level with no band (1 to 2) or no level at all stays unknown, so the memo's fallback applies; the Navigator-style category that `radar/frameworks.py` still finds is context only and sets no band. The criteria box, the verdict, the company page, Aava and `/api/company` therefore state one impact reading. Where the ladder puts impact at Medium on record, the fallback rules apply to it like the implied band, so more evidence never lowers a verdict (D53).

**The member rule and the code-alone cap (D65, D66).** The decision table is read in four passes: pass 0, a company declared in the Redstone Blue portfolio or the A'Pelago Nordic Blue cohort gets PORTFOLIO SUPPORT whatever its bands, and the sentence names the relationship and the next support (one pilot partner and one grant from the who-comes-next records, leaving out the organisation it already belongs to and any accelerator); pass 1, an exact match on all four cells; pass 2, the team's condition rules; pass 3, the fallback for High thesis fit with no impact evidence. The fallback runs only when the fit rests on more than the industry code: a fit whose every reason is the industry code or the register's own label for it is at most Medium ("the industry code alone gives at most a Medium fit"), while a fit reason from the source's own text, or a name word corroborated by a different source family, still gives High. On the home list of the cand/j4 build that gives WATCHLIST 31 and PORTFOLIO SUPPORT 28 (17 Oct, 22:40 UTC; it was WATCHLIST 30, PORTFOLIO SUPPORT 28 and PARTNER INTRODUCTION 1 at 17:38 UTC). PORTFOLIO SUPPORT is a rule about the partners' own companies, not a finding about them. Three later rules: one function, `memo.verdict_of`, decides the verdict for the company page, the match pages, the home list and Aava (D75); after the table, the startup cut keeps the accelerator verdict to companies founded at most 10 years ago that are not themselves a counterpart organisation (D83, D88), and a company that fails it stays on the watchlist; and a public contract sets business readiness, never technology readiness (D84), while its title gives a Medium thesis fit (D86).

## 8. Aava and the Ask Aava bar: how the words are made (by code; a reasoning mode exists and is off)

Aava (`radar/aava.py`) writes one first-person answer for each of six questions per company page ("Aava, what do you think about this company?", "What is their official score on the report?", "How sure are you?", "Who should we meet, and what do we say?", "What is the next move?", "What would change your mind?") and one for the feed ("Aava, what is new on the radar?"). Each answer is built by code from the rows the page also shows (the company record, its dated signals, the fund memo, the who-comes-next lists and the decision table). A fact the records lack is spoken as missing, for a declared cohort or portfolio member the answers name the member rule (D65), nothing is said about a named person, and nothing is read out that a listener cannot use (no web address, no record number, no rule id). Every segment carries a fingerprint of its text (`text_sha`), and a recording in `radar/static/aava/audio/` plays only when its manifest holds that fingerprint; otherwise the browser's own voice speaks the same text.

The Ask Aava bar (live, merge 5cd3293) adds a typed or spoken question and answers it from the same functions at `GET /api/ask?q=`. The router (`radar/ask.py`) tries, in order: "who should X meet" and "match X" (a company gets Aava's question 4 answer, an organisation the first startups of the match view's list with a link to it, D77), a company by name or business ID (Aava's question 1 answer and a link to the memo), filter phrases (verdict, country, sector, "TRL at least 4", which selects the filter bands 4 to 6 and 7 to 9, D72: the home filter rail's querystring and a count from the cached home list), top-startup questions ("top startups", "best startups", "top matches", "who should {org} invest in": the matchmaker ranking for A'Pelago or the organisation named, D63), "what is new", an organisation name, a partly matching company name, and otherwise the search page. Every word it reacts to is in the `ask:` block of the thesis file; there is no model and no network call. When a question matches nothing the answer says so and opens the search page; an example question is shown only when its answer is not empty in that sector's store (D40). A filter answer is applied in place on the home page (D32).

Question 6 ("What would change your mind?") lists only the changes that move the verdict up ("a new record on ocean impact could lift it: at high it would move up to due diligence"), or says that no single record would, and never says a record would lower or settle a verdict (D49); for a declared member it says what is missing and that the verdict rests on the declared member list, so only a change to that list would change it (D65); it re-reads the thesis file's own decision table with the impact taken from the ladder.

**The reasoning mode (D61, D62; live behind the demo flag since 17:37 UTC on 17 Oct).** When `ANTHROPIC_API_KEY` is in the app's environment and the file `data/AAVA_LLM_OFF` is absent, the bar and a company page's "Ask a follow-up" open a conversation with Aava on Claude Sonnet 5.5 (`radar/aava_llm.py`, `POST /api/aava/chat`, Server-Sent Events). The prompt is `config/aava_prompt.md` and says that tool text is data and a missing fact is a lead with the record that would settle it, never a low score. The model reasons only through read-only tools (`radar/aava_tools.py`; six are in the schema, and the handler of a seventh, `suggest_followups`, stays for a model that calls it, D62): `search_startups` (the cached home rows with the filter rail's own semantics), `get_startup` (the memo, the Ocean Impact Assessment, levels with evidence, dated signals with links, who to meet, the next-step rule), `explain_match`, `list_organisations`, `get_current_view`, `apply_filters` (validated, then queued as an action the browser applies; the server never runs it) and `suggest_followups`. Results are bounded (a long result is shortened row by row to valid JSON). After generation `radar/aava_entities.py` checks names and links: a company link stays only if a tool returned that company in the same turn and the label is its name, an external link only if its address appeared in a tool result, a plain mention of a returned company becomes a link, a company-shaped name no tool returned (and the visitor did not type) is left unlinked, logged and listed as unverified, and a Sources line is built from the addresses the tools cited. **What is not checked:** that a figure or date inside a sentence equals the record it restates. Any failure (no key, limit, timeout, API error, refusal) ends in a graceful sentence and the deterministic answer above. It is gated by the demo flag and limited to 500 characters of input, 12 messages of history, 8 turns per minute per client address and 400 turns per day. `python3 -m radar.aava_llm selftest "..."` runs one real turn: five of them took 4.7 to 7.0 s and cost $0.015 to $0.026 a turn (adaptive thinking at effort low, at most 3 steps; only the final turn's text is shown, D62). Answer quality across a question set is not scored.

## 9. Matchmaking

How a startup is matched to the counterparts that are its next step, and the other way round, with the practices adopted and rejected, the data, the rules (D28 to D30, A34) and the limits: [docs/MATCHING.md](docs/MATCHING.md). In one line: hard filters first (kind by verdict path, declared geography, open and inside its stated windows), then reasons in words with their records (sector or impact area, portfolio lookalike, shared words, readiness window, timing, geography), declared plus revealed demand, keywords read on both sides, and no score anywhere.

## 10. Sub-sectors: derived from evidence (D45)

The sub-sector filter's links are not labels we chose. `radar/sectors.py` applies the `taxonomy:` block of the thesis file (11 sub-sectors under "Blue economy": shipbuilding and boats, ship repair and maritime services, shipping and logistics, ports and marine construction, offshore and ocean energy, maritime software and tech, ocean data and monitoring, aquaculture and fisheries, marine biotech, water and pollution, coastal tourism and boating; a company with no link reads "Not yet classified") and writes one row per link to `company_sector` with its basis and the public page it rests on. Five bases: the industry code (a dot ignored; a gated code such as 5229 or 582 and 62 counts only together with a sector word in the company's own text), the company's own text (the registered line of business, the CORDIS title and objective, the BlueInvest profile, a quote the stitcher checked on a page, the verified brief; our own `detail` annotations are never read), a legal-name word backed by a record from a different source family (A5, A6), the declared ecosystem dataset's capability or a declared list's category, and a word in the title of a procurement notice the company won. Where the approved list was coarser than the data the narrower reading is used (ship repair is 3315, not 331, which also repairs machinery; boat retail is 47631, not all of 476; the stem "floating" is dropped for offshore energy because it sits in the name of ship-building code 30110). The backfill (`python3 -m radar.sectors backfill --db <store> --thesis <id>`) is idempotent and additive: no feed, memo, matching, readiness or decision rule reads the links.
