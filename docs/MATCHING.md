# Matchmaking: who is the next step for a startup, and which startups fit a counterpart

Built 17 Oct 2026 (branch `feat/matchmaking`; rules D28 to D30 and A34 in [PROJECT_RULES.md](../PROJECT_RULES.md)).
Code: `radar/matchmaker.py` (filters, components, ranking), `radar/matchmaker_orgs.py` (the counterpart side), `radar/matchtext.py`
(words, stems, keyword groups). Pages and API: `/match`, `/api/match/company/{key}`, `/api/match/org/{id}`, `/api/match/orgs`, and the
"Next steps" block on every company page, and the home page's tab "Top matching startups for {investor or accelerator}" (D35, default A'Pelago, `radar/home_orgs.py`), which prints `match_org`'s ranking, its first 25, the same list as the Match page (since D63 it is no longer intersected with the home list; the rail's other filters narrow it only through the facts a row carries), and shows each row's reasons in words under a line saying it is sorted by how well each startup matches the organisation; it changes no order, reason or threshold. Everything sector-specific (kinds, paths, regions, weights, keyword groups, stop words) is in
`config/thesis.yaml` under `matching:`; no sector word is in Python (a test fails if one is). Since the cand/j4 merge an investor's or accelerator's ranking offers startups only (section 3, D83 and D88), a closed call is never a next step (D76), and a startup's page carries a Weigh the reasons panel (section 3b, D90).

The data partner's rule for any ranking: it must explain itself in words ("Climate focus, Seed stage, Baltic region" is good; "AI score = 92"
is bad). Every listed counterpart therefore shows reason chips and, behind them, one sentence per satisfied reason with the record it rests
on. There is no total score for a startup in the page or in the match API, which returns an ordinal `rank` and a `weight` on each reason; `/api/company/{key}` carries the declared research dataset's own match `score` and its readiness numbers.

## 1. What the research said, and what we took

Researched 17 Oct (web search and fetched pages; the sources I could open are listed with what they say). Five practices adopted:

| # | Practice | Source | What we did with it |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Filter first, then rank.** Recommenders generate a small candidate set, then score it, then apply extra constraints; investor tools put stage, size and region in an elimination list before any scoring. | Google, [Recommendation systems overview](https://developers.google.com/machine-learning/recommendation/overview/types) ("generates a much smaller subset of candidates", then "scores and ranks the candidates"); Metal, [Recommended process](https://docs.metal.so/content/high-resolution-identification/recommended-process.md) ("build an elimination criteria ... a set of requirements that qualifies a given investor as a likely target") | Three hard filters (kind by verdict path, declared geography, stated window / closed) run before any reason is weighed. The counterpart view does the same in two passes: a cheap pass over every company, a full profile of the best 300 (the page says how many more passed the first pass and were not checked in full). |
| 2 | **Two-sided, mutual fit.** Event matchmaking and reciprocal recommenders look at both sides' conditions, and label whether a match is mutual or one-way. | b2match, [Event networking](https://www.b2match.com/news/give-your-attendees-the-networking-they-actually-want) ("Mutual interest" badge, "prioritize mutual matches first", each meeting "labelled so you know exactly why it exists"); [Reciprocal recommender systems: analysis of state-of-art literature](https://arxiv.org/abs/2007.16120) (arXiv 2007.16120) (RRS "need to take into account both users' preferences towards each other") | One component set serves both directions. The startup side contributes its verdict path, country, age, TRL, stage and words; the counterpart side contributes its declared conditions (geography, window, stage) and its demand. A pair is listed only if the startup's path admits the counterpart's kind and the counterpart's declared conditions admit the startup. |
| 3 | **Revealed preference from past deals, as proportions.** Investors concentrate on domains, and that shows in what they have funded; this also works for startups with no investor history yet. | Xu, Zhang, Lv, Mariani, [Recommending investors for new startups by integrating network diffusion and investors' domain preference](https://arxiv.org/abs/1912.02962) (34,469 investment events; "strong domain preferences"); Metal (investors "that have invested in similar companies can be an excellent starting point") | The lookalike component: how many of a counterpart's declared cohort or portfolio companies in our store share the startup's industry group, country and age band. It needs at least 2 such companies and 15% of the counterpart's declared companies in our records (our own threshold, D29: one in twenty is a coincidence). |
| 4 | **Explain every match; keep weights and cut-offs configurable and visible.** Matchmaking "cannot be a black box"; organisers tune weights and set minimum cut-offs so marginal suggestions are not surfaced. | InEvent, [AI matchmaking](https://inevent.com/products/ai/matchmaking.php) ("configurable weighting and thresholds", "Enterprise matchmaking cannot be a black box"); Converve, [B2B matchmaking software](https://www.converve.com/event-platform/b2b-event-matchmaking/) ("by your matching rules, not an AI black box"); Tintarev and Masthoff, [Explaining recommendations: design and evaluation](https://abdn.elsevierpure.com/en/publications/explaining-recommendations-design-and-evaluation/) (seven benefits explanations can give) | Reason sentences with links instead of a number; weights in `matching: weights`; a topic gate as the minimum cut-off (a place or a date alone is not a match). The weights order the list and are never shown as a total. |
| 5 | **Cold start: match on declared attributes and text, and weight words by rarity.** A new item or user has no history, so content features (descriptions, tags, metadata) carry the match; rare terms say more than common ones. | freeCodeCamp, [Cold start problem in recommender systems](https://www.freecodecamp.org/news/cold-start-problem-in-recommender-systems/) ("content-based filtering uses an item's metadata, such as features, categories or descriptions"); Stanford NLP group, Introduction to Information Retrieval, [Inverse document frequency](https://nlp.stanford.edu/IR-book/html/htmledition/inverse-document-frequency-1.html) ("the idf of a rare term is high, whereas the idf of a frequent term is likely to be low") | Every startup is a cold start (no interaction history), so the match runs on text and attributes: keyword groups from the thesis file for sector overlap, plus raw shared word stems. Stems found in more than 3% of the counterparts' texts or 1% of the startups' texts, and a stop list, are not evidence; two shared stems are needed, and the shared words are shown. |

Rejected, with the reason:

- **Learned scores, embeddings, LLM similarity.** Not explainable in words (the partner rule), and a paid API (project rule: no paid APIs).
- **Network diffusion / collaborative filtering** (Xu et al.'s method proper). It needs an investor-company event graph; our declared data has 67 cohort and portfolio links in the store, far too few. We kept their finding (domain preference is revealed by past deals), not the algorithm.
- **Aggregating two preference scores** (min, product, harmonic mean) as in reciprocal recommenders. We have no startup-side preference data, so there is no second score to combine; reciprocity is enforced by the filters instead.
- **Stable matching / capacity-aware scheduling** (RecSys-HR 2024 paper, b2match schedules). Counterparts declare no capacity or calendar, and the product recommends, it does not schedule.
- **Ticket-size matching** (in the partner's example: "Ticket size match"). A startup's funding ask is not in public records, so there is nothing to match against; no ticket reason is ever shown. Investors' declared tickets are kept in the registry for when an ask exists.
- **A single "match %"** (InEvent shows "95% Match"). Against the partner rule.

Not opened in full: Eventtia's matchmaking page (HTTP 404 on fetch), the RecSys-HR 2024 and KDD 2024 papers (seen in search results only). They are not cited as support.

## 2. The data

**Startups** (the store, 17,195 companies on the live copy of 17 Oct; 15,510 are matchable after leaving out ended or bankrupt companies and person-shaped legal names): country, founded date and age band, industry code and its family (first 3 digits, the NACE group, so ship building 301 is not aircraft 303), group tag, the four bands and the verdict (the same functions `radar/memo.py` uses; a test checks they equal `memo.build`), TRL, BRL, IRL and impact category (`radar/frameworks.py`), grant and pipeline signals. Own text = the registered line of business, the text of EU projects, research outputs, public contracts and similar signals (the radar's own annotations stripped), BlueInvest profile text, the brief's "what it does" sentence when one is stored, and, for the 73 startups in the ecosystem dataset, its description. Person names in free text are redacted (D17).

**Counterparts** (191 organisations, merged by folded name from `investors.csv`, `programmes_grants.csv`, `corporates_clients.csv`, the ecosystem dataset's accelerator, investor, public funder, cluster, corporate and port rows, and the programme windows in the thesis file): 57 investors, 16 accelerators, 11 grant or loan programmes, 32 funders, 60 corporates or public buyers, 10 ports, 5 clusters. Each has a demand profile in two parts.

- *Declared:* its own focus text as keywords and keyword groups, stages, geography (read into country codes through the `regions` table in the thesis file), ticket, TRL window, age limit, deadline, and the dataset's capability tags. 187 of 191 have declared text; 108 have a geography we can read; 4 have a dated deadline; 3 a TRL window; 13 are closed (11 before D76, which also closed the Interreg Central Baltic Programme 2021-2027 and the Business Turku Maritime Venture Program). A programme of an organisation is its own counterpart ("A'Pelago Northern Blue 2027"); a fund of an investor ("Redstone Blue Fund I") is merged into it.
- *Revealed:* the companies it has chosen that are in our store: the declared cohort and portfolio file (19 cohort companies to A'Pelago, 9 portfolio companies to Redstone Blue), the dataset's portfolio and investor tables, and the dataset's dated rounds, where the counterparty names a listed organisation (a counterparty that is not a listed organisation, such as a person, is dropped, never stored). 14 counterparts have at least one such company, 67 links in all. A programme reads lookalikes from the organisation that runs it but does not claim its cohort. An organisation with no declared text says so on the page: "its demand is revealed from its portfolio only".

The dataset's `person` table is never read, and neither are contact or "note" columns (D30).

## 3. How a match is made

**Hard filters** (D28): (1) *kind by verdict path*: ACCELERATOR, GRANT SUPPORT and WATCHLIST meet accelerators, programmes and public funders or foundations; PORTFOLIO SUPPORT (a declared cohort or portfolio member, D65) meets programmes, funders and clusters, and no accelerator, because it is already inside a programme; DUE DILIGENCE and INVEST meet investors; PARTNER INTRODUCTION meets clusters; REJECT / LOW FIT meets none; every path also meets corporates and ports as pilot partners. (2) *geography*: the place the counterpart declares must include the startup's country; a counterpart that declares nothing, or text with no readable place, is not filtered and the page says "no geography declared". (3) *window*: a closed programme, a passed deadline, a stated TRL window or age limit, or a stated stage window the startup is outside removes the counterpart. A counterpart that already selected the startup (its declared cohort or portfolio) is not a next step; it is shown as "Already connected". A programme whose call is closed (a declared deadline cell that says it is closed or names no open call, or a deadline before today) is never a next step; it is named only as "watch for the next call" (D76). **Investors and accelerators are offered startups only (D83, D88):** a startup here is a company founded at most 10 years ago (`geography.startup_max_age_years`, 10) that is not itself a counterpart organisation in the declared lists; a declared cohort or portfolio company is a startup by declaration. A company that is not a startup keeps its other next steps (corporates, ports, funders, programmes, clusters), and its own page says why no investor or accelerator is offered. An investor's or accelerator's page counts what it leaves out: 382 of the 15,510 matchable companies on the cand/j4 build (22:40 UTC), so 15,128 are offered. The page of any other kind (funder, programme, corporate, port, cluster) heads its list "Companies that fit", because a company of any age can work with it (D91). **For an investor (D63)** the same filter would show nothing, because no startup on the radar is at due diligence or invest yet (all 57 investors read "Best fits 0" in the judge's dry-run), so an investor is shown the early pipeline: the startups on the INVEST or DUE DILIGENCE paths first, then those on ACCELERATOR, GRANT SUPPORT and PORTFOLIO SUPPORT in the usual order, 1,000 candidates deep, under the sentence "No startup on the radar is at due diligence yet; these are the closest, in the order below." (or how many are). Each row says "not yet at due diligence" beside its path. A counterpart that ranks nobody prints why in words from the counts (a geography that excludes the country, no sector reason, nothing declared to match on).

**Reasons** (each a chip plus a sentence with links): *Sector or impact area* (the startup's industry code, a capability the declared ecosystem research lists for it, impact category or own text against the counterpart's declared words and listed capabilities, through the keyword groups; a capability is printed as its sector label, never as its id, D36); *Portfolio lookalike*; *Shared words* (raw word stems in both texts beyond the keyword groups, so one piece of evidence is not counted twice); *Readiness window* (TRL, age limit or stage inside what the counterpart states; a counterpart that states nothing is "not stated", never a miss); *Timing* (a stated deadline inside 90 days); *Geography*. A broad group (software, hardware) in common is shown but is never enough alone. A counterpart whose declared text names no sector (a grant open to any sector) is listed when the startup is inside its readiness window.

**Rank** (D29): by the weight of the satisfied reasons (`matching: weights`: sector 3, lookalike 3, shared words 2, readiness 2, timing 1, geography 1), then the number of reasons, then how much evidence stands behind the sector and word reasons, then the nearest deadline, then name. For a counterpart, its declared members are not ranked: they are its "Already connected" list (`connected` in the result, its own tab on the page), each with "Declared member" and its basis (D38); the ranking holds only the other startups, by the same rule; ties go to companies with a declared role or thesis evidence before bare register entries. A sentence of a declared description that is the list's own working note is neither shown nor read (D37), and generic words are in the stop list.

## 3b. Weigh the reasons (D90): the visitor's weights change the order

On `/match?company=<key>` a panel above the Next steps list lets a visitor weigh the reasons. It answers the owner's brief (the "Investor Matching Metrics Table": seven factors with weights, and a live ranking) without a total score: the weights order the list, each row shows which reasons hold, and no number or percentage stands beside a counterpart. Code: `radar/match_weights.py` (the factors, presets, ordering rule and the panel's data), `radar/routes/match_weights.py` (the panel's template function and `GET /match/weights/rows?company=<key>&skip=<n>`, which returns the counterparts below the shown cut as the same list rows), `radar/templates/_match_weights.html` and `radar/static/match_weights.js`.

| Factor in the brief | Brief's weight | Engine reason behind it | Matching weight in the thesis file | Slider value of the thesis file's weights (x 8) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Sector and thesis | 25 | Sector or impact area (`sector`) | 3 | 24 |
| Stage | 20 | Readiness window (`readiness`) | 2 | 16 |
| Geography | 15 | Geography (`geography`) | 1 | 8 |
| Ticket size | 15 | none: a startup's funding ask is not in public records, so the slider is drawn disabled and hatched ("Not in public records") and is never counted | none | none |
| Portfolio similarity | 10 | Portfolio lookalike (`lookalike`) | 3 | 24 |
| Recent activity | 5 | Timing (`timing`): an open deadline within 90 days | 1 | 8 |
| Strategic fit | 10 | Shared words (`keywords`) | 2 | 16 |

**The ordering rule**, in the browser: a counterpart's weight is the sum of the slider values over the reasons that hold (a reason with no record counts 0). The list is sorted by that sum, a tie keeps the server's order, and all sliders at zero keep the server's order. The page orders every counterpart that passes the filters, not only the shown ones (for Arctic Fry Oy it says "Orders all 29 counterparts that pass the filters and shows the top 12"), and the counterparts below the cut come from `/match/weights/rows`. **Presets:** The thesis file's weights are the page's start: the matching weights times 8 (the slider runs 0 to 50), so the order is exactly the server's. The brief's weights set 25, 20, 15, 10, 5 and 10 over the six scorable factors; Ticket size's 15 cannot be counted, so the six are rescaled: each slider shows its share of those six (the brief gives 29, 24, 18, 12, 6 and 12 percent, the thesis file 25, 17, 8, 25, 8 and 17, in the order Sector and thesis, Stage, Geography, Portfolio similarity, Recent activity, Strategic fit). Reset returns to the thesis file's weights. **The bars:** each row has a bar whose segments are its reasons, as wide as the weight you give them (holds solid, does not hold outlined, no record hatched), and a line naming what holds. A movement note such as "up 9" shows for 4 seconds, with no fade under reduced motion. The weights live in the URL (`&w=sector-25,readiness-20,geography-15,lookalike-10,timing-5,keywords-10`), so a link reproduces the order. Without JavaScript the panel is hidden and the server's order stands. An investor's or accelerator's view has no panel, because its early-pipeline order (D63) is not a sum of reasons.

**Measured** on the cand/j4 staging build (17 Oct, 22:45 to 22:55 UTC). With the thesis file's weights the order equals the server's for all 26 declared companies that have a list (computed with `match_weights.order` over each full list; 2 of the 28 have no counterpart). The brief's weights change the order for 2 of those 26: Emission-Eye Oy (11 of the top 12 move in the browser; Gasum Oy and Väylävirasto fall from 2nd and 3rd to 11th and 12th, because the brief gives Strategic fit less and Stage and Geography more) and VesselChain (6 of the top 12). For Arctic Fry Oy they move nothing: what holds for its 29 counterparts is Sector and thesis, Stage and Geography, in the same proportion. In headless Chromium on Arctic Fry's page, dragging Sector and thesis to 0 moves Business Finland Sprint Grant, which holds on Stage and Geography but not Sector, from 11th to 2nd ("up 9"), each of the nine counterparts above it falls one place, the line under the panel reads "10 of the top 12 moved", and the address becomes `&w=sector-0,readiness-16,geography-8,lookalike-24,timing-8,keywords-16`.

**Limits.** The brief's weights are the owner's, not fitted to anything; there is no ground truth for who should meet whom (section 5). Recent activity holds rarely, because only 4 of the 191 counterparts state a dated deadline (section 2). A weight cannot make a reason hold: it changes the order of the counterparts that passed the filters, never which ones pass.

## 5. Limitations (read before quoting a match)

- **No ground truth.** There is no list of which startup should have met which counterpart, so the weights are a stated hypothesis, not a fit. The words were tuned by reading junk matches on the live store (in-sample). A partner rating of 20 pairs would be the first real check (PILOT.md).
- **Thin text.** Most startups have only a registered line of business. 2,286 of 15,510 have any raw word evidence, 611 have any text beyond the register's description of the industry code, 11,258 have at least one sector group (mostly from the industry code). Shared words are the weakest reason and fire rarely.
- **Stage and TRL are mostly unknown.** Stage exists for the dataset's startups only; TRL is the radar's rule level, "not in public records" for most. Counterparts with a stated window therefore list few startups, and since D84 a public contract no longer gives a TRL, so a company with no TRL on record is not listed for a programme that states a TRL window (EIC Accelerator 2026, EU BlueInvest Investment Readiness Assistance).
- **Revealed demand is small:** 14 counterparts, 67 links. A lookalike is a share of a handful of companies.
- **Ticket size is not scored** (no ask on record).
- **Declared text is what the lists and the dataset say**, some of it unverified ("Thesis not verified", "Brief item"); low-confidence organisations are not hidden.
- **The counterpart view is approximate beyond its first pass:** the best 300 of a cheap first pass are profiled in full (an investor's view the best 1,000, D63). A startup whose only qualifying reason needs its TRL can be missed when 300 others outrank it on the first pass.
- **Programme windows and deadlines** come from the declared list and the thesis file as of 16 to 17 Oct; the 4 dated deadlines are not refreshed.
- A programme and the organisation that runs it can both appear (Business Finland and its Sprint grant).
- **The startup cut is a definition.** 10 years is a common definition of a startup (D88), not a measurement; the thesis file sets it, and it moves companies between investor lists (Sensmet Oy, registered 2017, is in at 10 years and was out at 5).
- Matchmaking is configured for the blue-economy thesis file only; the Baltic and health thesis files have no `matching:` weights, kinds or groups, and `/match` says so for them.
